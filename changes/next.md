- Your training history loads faster on the website and for AI assistants, however long you've been training.
- The app syncs straight to the server, not through the web proxy: faster to connect, and the server handles far more people at once. An older app is asked to update.
- Your AI assistant can see the workout you're doing right now, up to the set you just finished.
- On a new phone, your past workouts' heart rate charts show again.
- Usernames can't hold invisible characters, so nobody can pose as someone else with a name that looks the same, and a burst of failed sign-ins from one place is slowed down.
- Security tune-up across the app, watch and server: patched the building blocks they are made of and closed off ways the service could be abused. Nothing changes in how you use them.
- The app and watch work on the latest Android devices (the ones with larger memory pages); older builds could crash on them.
- Deleting your account now scrubs your workouts and heart-rate data from the server for good, not just from view.
- A sign-in that was already under way when a passkey is removed no longer goes through; the removed key stops working right away.
- Importing an account copy is safer: a tampered file that would balloon in size is refused, and switching accounts never shows the previous account's data by mistake.
- On the watch: signing out clears the workout the phone had shared, so a watch can never show the previous account's session, and the phone bounds what it holds from the watch so a flood of messages can't exhaust it.
- If an AI assistant's refresh token is used after it was already replaced (a sign it leaked), its access is cut off and it has to be reconnected.
- Behind the scenes, the app, watch, website and server moved to the latest build tools and libraries, so everything stays current, fast and secure. Nothing changes in how you use them.
- When a new lift is finding your starting weight, each ramp set now waits for you to say how it went: the next set doesn't load until you tap Easy or Hard. Easy takes a bigger jump up and a short 30-second rest; Hard gives you your normal rest before the next try.
- After a set, the countdown ring on its circle runs down much faster, so the banner moves on to the next set sooner, and it no longer shows while the "how was that?" question is up — the circle is still tappable there to take a rep off, so a ring would have been misleading.
- Deleting your account or adding a passkey now asks you to confirm with your passkey first, so an old session left open on its own isn't enough to do either.
- Coming back to the app (unlocking your phone between sets, switching apps, pulling down notifications) carries on with the connection it had instead of making a new one, so it's up to date at once; a connection that did drop is noticed within seconds and replaced.
