What the app will tell you to lift, and why

Every program in Schlift is a state machine: it proposes a session, watches what you actually do, and computes the next one. Pick a program and a situation to see the exact sequence it produces — including what happens when you stall, and when you disappear for six weeks.

The rules behind this

Time off

Under 14 days between sessions, nothing changes. From 14 days the next session is proposed at 90%; from 30 days, at 80%. There is no further step past 30 days.

The deload sticks

Coming back lighter is only a proposal until you train. Once you complete a session, that reduced weight becomes your working weight and you build from there — you are not snapped back to your pre-break load.

Missing reps

A session counts as hit only if you complete at least as many working sets as prescribed. Falling short holds the weight and increments a stall counter; extra sets you add yourself never count against you.

Failing never raises the bar

A missed session can never make the next one heavier. Whatever you attempted, the next prescription is at most what you just failed.

Progress follows what you lifted

Change a weight mid-session and the program follows the weight you actually finished on, not the number it originally asked for. Reordering your exercises does not confuse it.

Three strikes

On Stronglifts 5×5, a third consecutive stall on a lift triggers a 10% deload for that lift and resets the counter.
